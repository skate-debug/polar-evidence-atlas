# The physical and chemical stage for life at Earth's two poles

The Arctic and Antarctic are often paired as cold, ice-affected end members, yet their physical organization is almost inverse. The Arctic is an ocean nearly enclosed by continents, partitioned into broad shelves, deep basins, and submarine ridges and connected to lower latitudes through restricted Pacific and Atlantic gateways. Antarctica is a high continent surrounded by a circumpolar ocean whose uninterrupted zonal connection couples the Atlantic, Indian, and Pacific basins. This contrast—an ocean enclosed by land versus a continent enclosed by ocean—sets the boundary conditions for water-mass formation, nutrient delivery, biological isolation, and the routes by which climate anomalies propagate.

This framing is more than geographical description. At both poles, organisms encounter a moving mosaic of light, ice, salinity, temperature, substrate, redox state, and resource supply. Those conditions are produced by tectonic inheritance, bathymetry, cryospheric change, circulation, and biogeochemistry. Consequently, a useful account of polar viruses, prokaryotes, and eukaryotes must begin below and around the organisms: with the shape and history of the basins, the movement of water and ice, and the elemental resources that metabolism can access.

## Basin architecture and geological inheritance

Arctic bathymetry is both a geological archive and an active control on the ocean. Continental shelves occupy a large fraction of the basin, while the Lomonosov Ridge separates the Amerasia and Eurasia basins and modifies exchange between them. Sills, slopes, ridges, and gateways steer boundary currents, constrain deep-water pathways, and create sites of enhanced mixing. Yet less than one quarter of the Arctic seafloor has been mapped in detail, so geological reconstructions and the bathymetric boundary conditions used by circulation models retain substantial uncertainty [@Gaina2025ArcticBathymetry].

The two principal deep-basin systems have different and still debated histories. The Eurasia Basin records Cenozoic seafloor spreading along the Gakkel Ridge. Gakkel is an ultraslow-spreading end member in which magmatism, tectonic extension, crustal construction, and hydrothermal expression vary greatly along axis [@Nikishin2026GakkelRidgeReview]. The Amerasia Basin is more composite. Current syntheses integrate rotational opening of the Canada Basin, motions of Arctic Alaska–Chukotka, and construction of the Alpha–Mendeleev magmatic province, while emphasizing that sparse sampling beneath sea ice and sediment leaves several plate reconstructions viable [@Nikishin2025AmerasiaTectonics]. These uncertainties matter biologically because basin geometry governs circulation and because hydrothermal and weathering reactions create localized chemical energy sources.

Antarctica, in contrast, contains a long-lived continental crustal archive that is mostly hidden beneath the ice sheet. Sparse coastal outcrops preserve Archean through Proterozoic crustal evolution and major Cambrian reworking associated with Gondwanan assembly. Mineralogical, petrological, isotopic, and geochronological work provides the parent-material context for sediments and solutes ultimately delivered to polar habitats, but exposed rocks are a spatially selective sample of a largely inaccessible continent [@Hokada2024EastAntarcticGeology]. Airborne magnetic mapping around Dome Fuji illustrates how geophysics can extend that record beneath the ice: distinct magnetic domains and structural boundaries refine tectonic correlations, although their ages and compositions cannot be uniquely determined without rock samples [@Guy2024DomeFujiTectonics].

The Antarctic bed is also a hydrological landscape. More than 600 subglacial lakes have been identified, and repeated surface-elevation changes show that some fill and drain through dynamic networks. These connections can transport water and material, alter basal conditions, and influence ice motion. Direct-access projects at lakes such as Mercer and Whillans demonstrate that subglacial environments can be sampled, while also exposing the engineering and contamination-control challenges that distinguish direct observations from remote inference [@Zhou2024SubglacialHydrology]. Mercer Subglacial Lake, for example, contains a deeply isolated and genomically novel prokaryotic assemblage, including extensive Patescibacteria diversity [@Kim2025MercerMicrobiome]. The geology–hydrology–biology link is therefore literal: the form and movement of basal water define microbial habitat and connectivity.

## Circulation, ice, and freshwater

Arctic circulation reflects competition among relatively warm, saline Atlantic Water; fresher Pacific-origin water; river discharge; precipitation; sea-ice formation and melt; and exchanges through Fram and Bering straits and the Canadian Arctic Archipelago. Fresh surface water creates a strong halocline that can insulate sea ice from deeper Atlantic heat. At the same time, boundary currents carry heat and dissolved constituents along the continental slope. Bathymetry determines where those currents turn, mix, upwell, or exchange with the basin interior [@Gaina2025ArcticBathymetry; @Oziel2022ArcticSlopeNutrients]. As Atlantic influence expands, physical advection and environmental selection jointly reorganize the microbial community: ice-covered waters can retain comparatively stable resident populations, whereas stronger Atlantic influence and reduced ice favor more seasonally variable assemblages [@Priest2023BiologicalAtlantification].

Freshwater forcing also connects Greenland ice loss to basin-scale circulation. In paired EC-Earth3 ensembles under SSP5-8.5, a high-end Greenland runoff and calving trajectory added about 0.9 sverdrups per century of AMOC weakening through 2100 and approximately 4 sverdrups by 2300. The perturbation did not produce an abrupt or centennially irreversible transition in that model, but the single-model design and upper-end meltwater trajectory make this a mechanistic experiment rather than evidence that an AMOC tipping transition is impossible [@Mehling2026GreenlandMeltwaterAMOC]. Along Greenland's coast, the sign of local relative sea-level change can oppose the global mean because ice-mass loss and glacial isostatic adjustment raise the land and alter gravity. Near Aasiaat, community-guided bathymetry combined with solid-Earth modeling projects about 0.41-0.93 m of relative sea-level fall by 2100; shallow channel thresholds may therefore restrict navigation and coastal water exchange, although ecological and nutrient consequences remain projected rather than observed [@Tinto2026AasiaatRelativeSeaLevel].

The Southern Ocean is organized around the eastward Antarctic Circumpolar Current and a meridional overturning circulation that links surface, intermediate, deep, and abyssal waters. Relatively warm, nutrient- and carbon-rich Circumpolar Deep Water rises toward the surface and can access continental shelves. Around the coast, intense atmospheric cooling and sea-ice production increase shelf-water density. Antarctic coastal polynyas are particularly important: offshore winds repeatedly expose water, rapid ice formation rejects brine, and the resulting cold saline water contributes to dense shelf-water production [@Golledge2025AntarcticPolynyas]. When dense shelf water spills down the continental slope and mixes, it forms Antarctic Bottom Water, the lower limb that ventilates much of the global abyss.

This overturning is changing. Observations since the mid-1980s indicate broad warming, freshening, and contraction of Antarctic Bottom Water, with pronounced changes near formation regions. Meltwater and changing sea-ice production influence density and recent overturning strength, although sparse observations, regional differences, and incomplete model resolution prevent a single uniform circumpolar narrative [@Rintoul2025AntarcticBottomWater]. Event-scale freshwater perturbations can also be enormous. As iceberg A-68A decayed near South Georgia, observations and a melt budget indicated release of (152 \pm 61\) Gt of freshwater within 300 km of the island between November 2020 and March 2021. The event strengthened near-surface stratification while also producing localized vertical mixing, demonstrating that freshwater input can suppress and enhance vertical exchange in different parts of the same disturbance [@Lucas2025A68AMeltwater].

Southern Ocean changes also propagate beyond Antarctica. A hierarchy of coupled and slab-ocean experiments shows that tropical mean-state biases, particularly in the ITCZ and surface winds, can damp the modeled tropical response to Southern Ocean cooling by weakening wind-evaporation-sea-surface-temperature feedbacks [@Dong2026SouthernOceanTropicalBias]. Within the Antarctic system, the lagged statistical relationship between spring Southern Annular Mode variability and summer sea ice strengthened after the late 1990s through a stronger PSA-like circulation and Amundsen Sea Low pathway. The response is regionally heterogeneous and diagnostic rather than experimental, so it should not be reduced to one circumpolar SAM effect [@Dou2026SAMSummerSeaIce].

## Nutrient landscapes and geochemical constraints

Light is the most visibly seasonal polar resource, but sea-ice loss does not guarantee a proportional rise in production. Once light limitation relaxes, the supply and stoichiometry of nitrogen, phosphorus, silicon, iron, manganese, and organic substrates become decisive. The relevant supply routes differ sharply between the poles.

The Arctic receives intense land influence. Rivers deliver freshwater, dissolved and particulate nutrients, and organic carbon; coastal erosion adds material from thawing sediment and permafrost; and shallow shelves recycle nutrients before they reach the deep ocean. Model experiments estimate that rivers and coastal erosion sustain roughly 28–51% of present Arctic net primary production, with repeated shelf recycling amplifying the biological influence of terrestrial nitrogen [@Terhaar2021TerrestrialArcticNPP]. This result is a model attribution, not a direct tracer fraction, but it establishes land–ocean coupling as a central hypothesis for Arctic ecology.

Supply is spatially uneven. Although the continental slope occupies a small fraction of Arctic area, eddy-permitting simulations identify it as a disproportionate conveyor of dissolved inorganic nitrogen. Intensifying lateral eddy flux and upwelling can raise nutrient supply along the slope even while renewal weakens in the central basin. Thus, declining ice can produce adjacent regions of enhanced nutrient transport and stronger nutrient limitation [@Oziel2022ArcticSlopeNutrients]. On the Siberian shelves, nitrogen removal adds another control. A 1998–2022 reconstruction attributes at least half of Arctic benthic denitrification to the broad, shallow Siberian shelf system and finds a rapid increase after the 2009 sea-ice regime transition, concurrent with reduced nitrogen supply in Pacific-origin waters [@SantosGarcia2026ArcticNitrogenRegimeShift]. The implied transition from predominantly light-controlled toward increasingly nutrient-controlled production occurred on a timescale of only a few years in the model, but its causality and pan-Arctic reach should not be overstated.

Arctic weathering also couples cryospheric change to river chemistry. In the Koyukuk watershed, sulfate isotopes and inverse modeling attributed approximately 80% of river sulfate to sulfide oxidation. Rising sulfate and magnesium over longer records are consistent with deeper or newly exposed flow paths as permafrost changes, although lithology, hydrology, and source end members differ among watersheds [@Kemeny2023ArcticSulfideWeathering]. Such reactions matter because sulfide oxidation can alter alkalinity and carbon-cycle feedbacks on timescales very different from immediate biological uptake.

Thaw also changes which microorganisms grow and what carbon remains degradable after transport. In one Bayelva active-layer core, quantitative stable-isotope probing separated early and late post-thaw growers: only 22% of labeled sequence variants grew at both 21 and 98 days, while predatory and epibiotic lineages also incorporated label [@Cramm2026HighArcticThawGrowth]. A linked Svalbard multi-omics comparison found carbohydrate-degradation capacity and expression in both active-layer soil and fjord surface sediment. This shows that some buried land-derived organic matter remains biologically available, but it does not quantify the fraction remineralized across an entire fjord [@Abuah2026SvalbardCarbohydrateDegradation].

New 2026 observations sharpen two interfaces in this land–ice–ocean system. Along a Ny-Ålesund glacier–foreland–fjord continuum, 309 bacterial MAGs showed that glacial lineages remained common in the foreland but were rare in Kongsfjorden, where endemic marine lineages dominated; release from retreating ice therefore did not imply successful coastal establishment [@Liu2026GlacierMicrobialFiltering]. At a West Greenland landfast-ice site near marine-terminating glaciers, bottom-ice algal production reached 146 ± 4.8 mg C m^-2 d^-1. Estimated turbulent silicic-acid supply matched the bloom's inferred demand, but the one-site, one-date design makes the proposed glacier–circulation–nutrient mechanism a focused hypothesis rather than a general Arctic rule [@Sogaard2026GlacierIceAlgae].

The open Southern Ocean receives less direct river input and is famous for high macronutrients but comparatively low chlorophyll over much of its area. Iron limits or co-limits phytoplankton growth across many offshore waters, yet treating iron as the only trace-metal constraint is inadequate. Trace-metal-clean incubations show that, at some stations, combined iron and manganese addition stimulates greater phytoplankton growth and macronutrient drawdown than iron alone [@Browning2021ManganeseColimitation]. The result is local and experimental, but it changes the conceptual model from one universal limiting element to a spatially variable, multielement nutrient landscape. Iron and manganese may arrive through sediments, upwelling, ice and iceberg melt, atmospheric deposition, and biological recycling; their chemical form and residence time are as important as total concentration.

Phase-resolved measurements across the Amundsen Sea now illustrate that complexity directly. Total iron, copper, zinc, and lead were enriched in the polynya, whereas the sea-ice zone showed selective iron enrichment; dissolved and particulate pools often followed different regional patterns. Five-day iron additions produced little additional chlorophyll accumulation in the polynya, a clearer offshore response, and a highly variable response in the sea-ice zone [@Ge2026AmundsenTraceMetals]. Sea ice also concentrates organic sulfur resources: during the 2022 austral winter SCALE cruise, total DMSP was 2–38 times higher in ice than in seawater, while size-fractionated metagenomes linked algal and bacterial biosynthetic genes with abundant bacterial demethylation and cleavage potential [@Buthelezi2026SeaIceDMSP]. Neither gene abundance nor standing-stock concentration alone establishes process rate or atmospheric sulfur flux.

These physical and chemical constraints determine the efficiency—not only the magnitude—of biological carbon cycling. Southern Ocean biota fix, transform, respire, and export a globally important quantity of carbon, but the fraction stored at depth depends on community composition, grazing, microbial recycling, particle properties, and circulation [@Boyd2024SouthernOceanCarbon]. Coastal sediment traps along the western Antarctic Peninsula show why seasons outside the summer bloom matter: autumn particulate-organic-carbon export can rival the local summer pulse, with diatoms and euphausiid fecal pellets dominating collected particles [@Isla2025WAPParticleFlux]. In the Arctic, a model that includes river and erosion inputs projects an approximately 40% decrease in biological-carbon-pump efficiency by 2100 under its analyzed scenario, while terrigenous carbon and coastal outgassing offset part of the oceanic carbon sink [@Oziel2025ArcticCarbonPump]. These results use different spatial domains and methods and should be compared as mechanisms, not as directly interchangeable flux estimates.

## From Earth system to biological knowledge graphs

The environmental template becomes biologically meaningful through time and habitat. At Rothera, a paired viral and cellular metagenomic series revealed thousands of mostly catalogue-unrepresented vOTUs with pronounced seasonal succession [@Piedade2024AntarcticViralSeascape]. At a Central Arctic observatory, viral communities recurred annually and tracked prokaryotic community structure across four years [@Calayag2025ArcticVirusSeasonality]. A new comparison of nine Arctic, Baltic, and Antarctic sea-ice metagenomes recovered 550 vOTUs and predicted hosts for 187, but heterogeneous sampling and bulk 0.2-µm filtration limit biogeographic and free-virus inference [@Pettersson2026PolarSeaIceViruses]. Culture work adds mechanisms that metagenomes alone cannot resolve: two independently replicating viruses hidden within one long-maintained *Micromonas polaris* lysate showed opposite competitive rankings in nutrient-replete dual infection and Arctic environmental read recruitment [@Meyer2026ArcticMicromonasViruses].

Sea-ice geometry and melt stage create additional ecological structure. MOSAiC pressure-ridge interiors contained compositionally distinct void, ice-block, and bottom-ice communities; local geometry scaling suggested that ridges occupying 22% of surveyed ice area could hold 80 ± 20% of ice-associated chlorophyll in July [@Muller2026ArcticSeaIceRidges]. Near Utqiaġvik, highly porous rotten ice had lost many vertical dissolved gradients but retained upper-ice particulate material and a restructured microbiome [@Frantz2026RottenSeaIce]. A 49-hour Fram Strait melt experiment then traced approximately one-tenth of bacterial and microeukaryotic taxa, as well as a subset of ice-specific organic molecular formulae, into meltwater [@CardozoMino2026SeaIceMeltExport]. These studies collectively identify ridge interiors, advanced melt ice, and the meltwater boundary as separate graph habitats; none alone provides a pan-Arctic biomass or export flux.

Across lakes, 17 benthic mats from Arctic, Antarctic, and sub-Antarctic settings shared stress-response and phosphorus-cycling potential while retaining strong environment-associated functional differences [@DeVisscher2026BipolarLakeMats]. A circumpolar Southern Ocean gene survey found water-mass identity to be a dominant organizer of prokaryotic gene composition [@Faure2026SouthernOceanGenes]. Eukaryotes also respond to rapid physical regime shifts: long-term pigment syntheses show regionally and seasonally different changes among diatoms, haptophytes, and cryptophytes as Antarctic sea ice moved into a low-extent state [@Hayward2025AntarcticPhytoplankton; @Adroli2026SouthernOceanRegimeShift]. In the Ross Sea Region Marine Protected Area, 101 multi-depth eDNA samples collected in 2024–2025 detected 92 species in both surveys and associated community separation with thermal stratification and water masses; because eDNA records molecular presence rather than abundance or activity, it complements rather than replaces organismal and rate measurements [@Choi2026RossSeaEDNA].

Together these studies argue against a graph made only of taxon–location links. The useful unit is a scoped evidence claim: a taxon, process, or water mass connected to a driver or habitat, with location, season, method, inference type, and limitation retained. Genomic potential must remain distinguishable from a measured rate; co-occurrence from infection; model projection from observation; local perturbation from circumpolar trend. The accompanying knowledge graph follows that rule, allowing the physical narrative introduced here to connect directly to viruses, prokaryotes, and eukaryotes without erasing how each relationship was established.
